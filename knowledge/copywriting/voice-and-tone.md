# Voice and tone

## Voice

Sound like a mischievous editor letting a friend in on a recurring habit. Keep observations concrete, jokes brief and claims qualified. British English is a reversible project default.

Proposed opening: “Some writing habits have a reputation. We keep the receipts.”

Proposed entry introduction: “The dramatic pivot. A sentence puts one idea on trial, then unveils its grand replacement.”

Use plain evidence labels: “Reported association”, “Studied in this setting”, “Date unknown”. On every pattern page: “This pattern does not establish AI authorship.” These are draft examples for tone review, not approved site copy.

## Tone by situation

Browse pages can wink. Evidence notes, corrections and uncertainty use direct factual language. A deprecated entry might say: “Retired from the current guide. Here is what changed.” A failed scout says: “Three sources could not be checked. Existing entries are unchanged.”

## Review practice

For every public copy change, load the current approved rules export, flag relevant habits, read each passage in context and record intentional exceptions in review notes. Reword unsupported superlatives into specific supported statements. Avoid reflexive contrast formulas, inflated emotional claims and repetitive dramatic punctuation in the site's own prose.

These are editing prompts, not evidence of authorship. Retain a useful em dash or contrast when it serves the sentence. Do not rewrite cited quotations to satisfy house style. Check meaning and source fidelity after every edit. Revisit the guide when accepted evidence or reader feedback changes it; never silently let a generated rule override editorial judgement.

## Future export

Generate `rules.md`, structured rules JSON and an optional `SKILL.md` from approved pattern records rather than maintaining competing copies. Each rule needs a stable ID, version, applicability, example, suggested alternative, exceptions and evidence links. State that the skill supports editing and cannot identify authorship. Resolve licensing before distributing quoted material or accepting contributions.
